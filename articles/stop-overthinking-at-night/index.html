How to stop overthinking at night

A 5-minute evening check-in · Ger Boland · 28 November 2026

You get into bed tired, and then your mind switches on. The conversation you wish had gone differently. The email you still haven’t answered. Tomorrow’s list. An hour later you’re still awake, going round the same loop.

Overthinking at night is very common, and it’s rarely because something is wrong with you. During the day you’re busy, so thoughts get parked. At night, with nothing else to do, they all come back at once. The fix isn’t to fight the thoughts. It’s to give them somewhere to go before you lie down.

The 5-minute evening check-in

Do this at the same time each evening, ideally away from your bed: at the kitchen table, on the couch, with a cup of something warm. Paper is fine. So is your phone, as long as you put it down afterwards.

1. Close the day (1 minute)

Write down three things that happened today, in one line each. Not a diary, just a quick record. This tells your brain the day has been “filed”, so it doesn’t need to keep replaying it.

2. Empty the loop (2 minutes)

Write every thought that’s still nagging you, as fast as you can, without sorting it. Worries, to-dos, half-finished conversations. Then, next to anything you can actually act on, write one small next step and when you’ll do it.

“Reply to Mary” becomes “Reply to Mary, 10am tomorrow, two lines.”

Anything you can’t act on, you can simply notice: “That’s out of my hands tonight.”

3. One kind sentence (1 minute)

Overthinking often comes with a harsh inner voice. Write one sentence you’d say to a friend who had your day. “You did a lot today. The rest can wait until morning.” It feels awkward at first, and that’s fine.

4. Set tomorrow’s first priority (1 minute)

Choose just one thing that would make tomorrow feel good. When you know where you’re starting, your mind has less reason to plan at 2am.

If the thoughts come back in bed

They might, especially for the first week. When a thought shows up, remind yourself: “It’s written down. I’ll deal with it at the time I chose.” Then bring your attention to your breathing: in for four, out for six, a few times. A longer out-breath helps your body slow down.

Give it a week or two. Like any habit, the evening check-in works best when it’s short and regular, not perfect.
